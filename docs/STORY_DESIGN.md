# Two Rooms, One Tide: Story and Puzzle Design

Status: draft for review. Nothing in the code or assets has been changed yet. Names, lines and numbers are all open to edits.

---

## 1. Logline, tone and the Triangle

**Logline.** On a lighthouse at the edge of the Triangle, a disgraced signals officer and a keeper's apprentice are locked in two rooms with a dead man's secrets between them. Together they expose the harbourmaster who has been wrecking ships for insurance. Then one last line of soot on the lamp lens suggests that one of them killed the keeper, and the player is the one who wiped half of it away.

**Tone.** Quiet, melancholic and close: a storybook palette with a noir heart. Two voices in a speaking tube, a storm outside, small mechanical sounds. No gore and no jump scares. The dread comes from re-reading things you thought you understood.

**Setting.** Gull Rock Light, a reef lighthouse in the western Atlantic, 1928. The lamp is electric and powered by a tide wheel in the cellar. Elias lit it first in 1874, which is why the existing drawer code still works.

**Myth versus man-made.** Sailors call the sea around the rock "the Triangle": compasses wander, lights vanish and ships go down with nobody to blame. Nine ships have been lost on the reef in three years. The game's answer is that the Triangle is a man with a pen. On chosen nights the lamp is turned low, ships hit the reef, the cargo is salvaged and the insurance is paid. Harbourmaster Crane runs it. The myth survives as a cover story everyone is happy to repeat. One small detail is left unexplained on purpose: Elias's desk compass never settles. It does nothing for the plot and it keeps the legend alive.

(The phrase "Bermuda Triangle" was coined in 1964, so in-world characters just say "the Triangle". The jam page and title card can say "Bermuda Triangle" freely.)

## 2. Characters

### Mara Quell (lamp room)
- **Who:** Coastguard signals officer, early 30s, dismissed for negligence. On the night the Marigold sank she logged "no distress signal seen". Her younger brother Danny was a deckhand on the Marigold.
- **Stakes:** Her name, and the truth about Danny.
- **Skill:** Lights and Morse. She reads Elias's flash-code shorthand without effort, and she is the one who signals the cutter at the climax.
- **Secret:** Elias wrote to her confessing he had a hand in the Marigold. She rowed out on Thursday, the night he died, and argued with him on the balcony. She says he was alive when she left. She tells Tobin she has never been here before.
- **Voice:** Clipped and procedural, with dry humour. Short sentences and radio habits ("Say again." "Copy.").
- **Sample lines:** "There's no Triangle. There's a man with a pen." / "Then it's lucky it's me touching it."

### Tobin Ashby (cellar)
- **Who:** Elias's apprentice, mid 20s. Round glasses, mustard sweater, always carrying the small engine diary he keeps for the tower.
- **Stakes:** Elias was the nearest thing he had to a father. As the only other person who lived on the rock, he is the obvious suspect, and he could lose the only home he has.
- **Skill:** Machines. Valves, generator, rheostat, gaskets. He knows every bolt in the tower.
- **Secret:** On "rough nights" Elias told him to turn the lamp low, to rest the generator. Elias passed him envelopes he took to be wages. He also says he was in town on Thursday. His own diary has an entry in his hand from 11:40 that night.
- **Voice:** Warm and rambling. He interrupts himself, quotes Elias and apologises too much.
- **Sample lines:** "He called me T in his diary. One dash. Shortest letter for the shortest lad on the rock." / "I serviced every bolt in this tower, Mara."

### Elias Venn (dead; present through his writing)
A keeper for 54 years, careful and guilty. He was drawn into Crane's scheme and wanted out. He writes names in flash-code (Morse) in his log, a habit the player learns in the first minute and that pays off in the last. He scratched a final warning into the soot on the lens rim: "IF I FALL IT WAS" followed by a name in code.

### Harbourmaster Silas Crane (offstage)
He really is guilty of wreck fraud, and the photograph and ledger prove it. He is the satisfying culprit the story hands over. His only line, read out at the inquest in the ending text, is "I never set foot on that rock. Ask the two who did."

Danny Quell, the ship *Halcyon* (due tonight) and the cutter *Vigilant* are mentioned but never shown.

### How the two leads stay equally interesting
Each one gets a skill that the other room needs, a personal loss, one lie told in the first conversation, one optional concealment choice, one required tampering act performed by the player, and one entry in Crane's ledger. Every suspicious moment has a believable innocent reading.

| # | Character | Suspicious moment | Innocent reading | Guilty reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mara | Elias's log says "Tell **−−** everything Thursday" (M in Morse). | He was going to confess the Marigold to her. | He confessed and she took revenge for Danny. |
| 2 | Mara | Her own letter in his drawer: "Thursday. If it's true, God help you. — M." Player chooses to hide it or mention it. | Grief and anger, not a threat. Hiding it is self-protection. | A threat, followed by a cover-up. |
| 3 | Mara | A tuft of red wool on the balcony rail he fell from. | They argued there and the scarf snagged. She left him alive. | Struggle at the rail. |
| 4 | Mara | **Tampering:** she wipes the soot off the lens with her scarf. | She has to clean it so the projection is readable. | She knew what was written there. |
| 5 | Mara | Ledger: "M. — no signal logged, Marigold." | Crane offered a bribe and logged it hopefully. She refused. | She was paid to look away. |
| 6 | Tobin | Says he was in town Thursday. His diary says "Thurs 11:40 gen. checked" in his hand. | He came back to check the generator and is ashamed he wasn't there to help. | He was there when Elias died. |
| 7 | Tobin | Envelope in the locker marked "T." Player chooses to hide it or mention it. | Elias handed him "wages" and he never asked. | Crane paid him directly. |
| 8 | Tobin | Goes straight to the rheostat, which is set to the scratched "LOW" mark. | He services it every week. | He is the one who turned it low. |
| 9 | Tobin | **Tampering:** tears a diary page headed "Low nights — E's orders" to make a gasket. | He needs a gasket now, and it's the paper he has. | He destroys the only proof of who gave the orders. |
| 10 | Tobin | **Tampering:** his valve flood washes away two sets of boot prints by the stairs. | Flooding is how the tide wheel works. | Convenient. |
| 11 | Tobin | Ledger: "T. — low nights — £40." | Elias split his own payments. | He was Crane's man in the tower. |

The pattern is deliberate. Each player-performed tampering destroys exactly the evidence that could have cleared or damned that character. The player does it for a good reason and only understands it at the end.

## 3. Story structure

### Beat sheet
1. **Opening (0–1 min).** Title card, then the intro. Mara wakes to the barred lamp-room door and Tobin to the barred cellar stairs. A ship is due on the evening tide.
2. **Investigation (1–7 min).** Logbook, chalk, drawer and locker. The first speaking-tube talk gives both alibis, which are both lies. Mara finds her letter and Tobin finds his envelope.
3. **Working the tower (7–12 min).** The fuse is fitted but there's no power. The lens projection goes down the weight-well. Mara wipes the lens. Tobin makes a gasket from his diary, then sets the valves. The cellar floods and the evidence floats up.
4. **Midpoint reveal (≈12 min).** The photograph of Crane watching the Marigold sink. Mara's brother. They have a villain.
5. **Rising tension (12–15 min).** The lamp lights but runs dim, because someone set it low. Tobin knows where the rheostat is. Mara reads the ledger and both their initials are in it. They choose to believe each other.
6. **False resolution (15–17 min).** Mara taps CRANE (or SOS) to the cutter in Morse. The cutter answers. An ending card tells what happens next: Crane in irons, or a quiet cover-up.
7. **Twist (17–19 min).** "But that was later." The lamp runs at full power for the first time, lighting the whole lens including the rim. Elias's soot message is thrown onto Tobin's wall: IF I FALL IT WAS **−** and then a clean streak where the scarf went. One dash is T. Two dashes is M.
8. **Open final frame.** Whoever the player is controlling clicks the hatch, and the *other* character says the last line. Cut to black.

### Draft text

**Intro (replaces `INTRO`)**
> Atlantic, 1928. Sailors call this stretch of sea the Triangle: compasses wander, ships vanish, and nobody is ever to blame. Gull Rock Light stands on its western edge. Nine ships have been lost on the reef below it in three years.
>
> Three nights ago the keeper, Elias Venn, was found on the rocks at the foot of the tower. Harbourmaster Crane called it a fall.
>
> Tonight two people rowed out to find out why the light keeps going dark: Mara Quell, a coastguard signaller dismissed after the Marigold went down, and Tobin Ashby, Elias's apprentice. Someone barred the doors behind them. Mara is in the lamp room. Tobin is in the cellar. A ship is due on the evening tide.

**Speaking-tube conversations** (played at the hatch; a pending call makes the hatch glow)

*T1. First contact (after both characters have looked around)*
> TOBIN: Mara? The tube still works. Elias used to shout the tea order down it.
> MARA: I hear you. Barred up here too.
> TOBIN: Crane's men. They must have watched us row out.
> MARA: You said you were in town Thursday. The night he died.
> TOBIN: At my aunt's. ...Why?
> MARA: Getting the times straight. I'd never been out here before tonight.

*T2. Fuse fitted, no power*
> MARA: Fuse is in. Nothing.
> TOBIN: There wouldn't be. The lamp runs off the tide wheel, and the valves down here feed it. The paint's gone off the tags. Elias said he "set them by the light". I never knew what he meant.
> MARA: Did he trust you with anything?
> TOBIN: Everything. He called me T in his diary. One dash. Shortest letter for the shortest lad on the rock.

*T3. Smeared projection*
> TOBIN: There's light on the wall down here! A glass plate by the chalk. It's just a smudge, like looking through a thumbprint.
> MARA: The lens is black with soot. I'll clean it.
> TOBIN: Careful. He never let me touch that glass.
> MARA: Then it's lucky it's me touching it.

*T4. Drawer and locker (built from the two concealment choices)*
> If Mara mentioned the letter: MARA: There was a letter in his drawer. Mine. I was meant to come out Thursday. / TOBIN: ...Did you? / MARA: He was alive when I left.
> If she hid it: TOBIN: Anything in the drawer besides the key? / MARA: A pencil stub.
> If Tobin mentioned the envelope: TOBIN: There's money in the locker. An envelope with a T on it. I don't know why. / MARA: Crane pays people, Tobin. It's what he does.
> If he hid it: MARA: Anything in the locker besides the fuse? / TOBIN: Rags. Just rags.

*T5. Evidence found*
> TOBIN: Under the floor. A photograph of Crane on the dock, watching a ship go down. The stern says Marigold.
> MARA: ...My brother was on the Marigold.
> TOBIN: I'm sorry. There's a ledger too. Payouts for every ship the papers blamed on the Triangle.
> MARA: There's no Triangle. There's a man with a pen.

*T6. Lamp lit but dim*
> MARA: It's lit, but weak. Orange, like a candle in a jar.
> TOBIN: The rheostat'll be on low. It's on the pipe by the wheels. Hang on.
> MARA: You knew exactly where that was.
> TOBIN: I serviced every bolt in this tower, Mara.

*T7. Ledger read (after it is sent up)*
> MARA: There's a T in this ledger. Forty pounds. "Low nights."
> TOBIN: And an M. "No signal logged, Marigold." What's that?
> MARA: Crane offered. I said no. The light was dark; there was nothing to see.
> TOBIN: Elias told me to turn it low on rough nights. I thought it was wages.
> MARA: Then we believe each other.
> TOBIN: ...Yes.

**Final scene**
> (Plate, as Tobin) The full beam lights the whole lens now, rim and all. Words fall down the well backwards in Elias's scratchy hand. You read them the right way round: IF I FALL IT WAS. Then one long bar. After it, a clean streak where the soot was wiped away.
> (Notebook adds) Flash-code: T is one dash. M is two.
> (Lens, as Mara) A long clean streak across the rim, the width of a scarf.
> (Scarf, as Mara) Soot ground into the red wool.

Last line, chosen by who is clicking the hatch:
> As Tobin: MARA: "Tobin? The boat's here. ...Leave the wall. It's only soot."
> As Mara: TOBIN: "Mara? The boat's here. ...You'll want to wash that scarf."

Cut to black. Card: *The tide went out at six.* Then the title.

### Ending variants
The ending card plays after the signal and before the final scene. It has three layers: which signal was sent, a tide-band line and small concealment lines.

**Truth (CRANE signalled; needs the ledger in Mara's hands)**
> The cutter answers: CRANE. UNDERSTOOD. By noon the harbourmaster is in irons and the Marigold is no longer a mystery of the Triangle. At the inquest Crane says only one thing: "I never set foot on that rock. Ask the two who did."

**Cover (SOS only)**
> The cutter answers and lowers a boat. The report says the lamp failed and the keeper fell. On the dock Harbourmaster Crane shakes their hands and thanks them for their courage. He holds Mara's a moment too long.
> Footer: *There is another ending. Proof was waiting to be sent up.*

**Tide line** (tide level when the lamp reached full power)
- 0–2: "The *Halcyon* turned away with a mile of clear water to spare."
- 3–4: "The *Halcyon* scraped the outer rocks and limped home. Every hand was saved."
- 5–6: "The *Halcyon* broke on the reef. The cutter took off her crew; the sea took her cargo. Crane's last payout." (In Truth: "...the one payout he never collected.")

**Concealment lines** (added only when the item was hidden)
- Letter hidden: "Mara's letter stays in her coat. Nobody asks for it."
- Envelope hidden: "Tobin's envelope stays in his pocket. Nobody asks for it."

## 4. Puzzle chain

### Mechanics summary
1. **Cross-room information.** The chalk code goes to the drawer. The chalk anchor goes to the lens dial. The lens projection goes to the valves. The generator powers the lamp. The rheostat sets the beam. The final projection carries the twist.
2. **Hands-on mini-puzzles.** Lens dial (with the wipe), valve wheels and the Morse shutter.
3. **Speaking tube.** Seven short talks at the hatch (section 3).
4. **Tampering.** Lens wipe (Mara), diary gasket (Tobin), flood washing away the prints (Tobin), plus two concealment choices (letter, envelope).
5. **Tide clock.** Six levels with no fail state. Covered at the end of this section.

### Dependency flow

| Step | Who / room | Action | What crosses rooms | Flags set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mara / lamp | Read logbook. Last entries: "**−·−·**'s men on the rocks again" (Mara: "C. Crane."), "Tell **−−** everything Thursday", "The drawer code is chalked below", "The well flips everything". | Teaches flash-code names. | `read_logbook` |
| 2 | Tobin / cellar | Read chalk: "LIT 1874 — drawer" and a chalk anchor with "to the well". | Code and anchor go up (player memory, notebook). | `knows_code`, `knows_anchor` |
| 3 | Either | Hatch: talk T1. | Story | `talk_1` |
| 4 | Mara / lamp | Drawer keypad 1874. Get key and letter, then choose **Keep it to yourself** or **Tell Tobin**. | | `drawer_open`, `letter_hidden` or `letter_told` |
| 5 | Mara → Tobin | Send key down. | Item | `key_sent` |
| 6 | Tobin / cellar | Locker with key. Get fuse and envelope "T.", then choose **Pocket it** or **Tell Mara**. | | `locker_open`, `envelope_hidden` or `envelope_told` |
| 7 | Tobin → Mara | Send fuse up. Mara fits it: no power. Talk T2 (and T4 once both choices are made). | Item | `fuse_fitted` |
| 8 | Mara / lamp | Click lamp to open the **Lens dial**. Mara hangs her lantern in the lens cage. Light goes down the weight-well. | Light to the cellar | `lantern_set` |
| 9 | Tobin / cellar | Glass plate: a blurred smear. Talk T3. | | `saw_smear` |
| 10 | Mara / lamp | Lens dial: **Wipe with scarf** (tampering 1), then rotate the anchor panel under the WELL pointer. | Projection to the cellar | `lens_wiped`, `lens_set` |
| 11 | Tobin / cellar | Glass plate: five coloured dots with a backwards "1" at the right end. | Valve order (mirrored) | `knows_order` |
| 12 | Tobin / cellar | Valves: wheel three sprays. Use diary on valves to tear the "Low nights" page into a gasket (tampering 2). | | `gasket` |
| 13 | Tobin / cellar | **Valve wheels** mini-puzzle. Correct: cellar floods, tide wheel turns, prints wash away (tampering 3). Wrong: tide +1. | Power to the lamp | `valves_set` |
| 14 | Tobin / cellar | Floating plank: photo and ledger. Talk T5. | | `evidence_found` |
| 15 | Mara / lamp | Lamp: lit, but dim (orange tint). Talk T6. | | `lamp_lit` |
| 16 | Tobin / cellar | Rheostat (visible after step 15): turn from LOW to FULL. | Beam strength up | `lamp_full`; balcony unlocks |
| 17 | Mara / lamp | Window: the *Halcyon* and the cutter. Balcony: red wool on the rail (optional). | | `saw_ship`, `saw_wool` |
| 18 | Tobin → Mara | Send ledger (and photo) up. Mara reads it: talk T7. | Item | `ledger_sent` |
| 19 | Mara / lamp | Lamp: **Morse shutter**. Choose CRANE (needs ledger) or SOS. | Signal out | `signalled_truth` or `signalled_sos` |
| 20 | Both | Ending card, then the final scene: auto-switch to Tobin, plate, optional switch to Mara (lens, scarf), hatch line, black. | Twist | `final_seen` |

Optional flavour that never gates anything: the crate holds an oversized oilskin glove with Crane's harbour stamp, which supports the Crane reading. The stairs show the boot prints until the flood. The desk compass never settles.

Steps 2, 4 and 8 can run in any order, as can 9–12 and 14–15. Every room needs the other at least three times: steps 2→4, 2→10, 10→11 and 13→15.

### Mini-puzzle A: Lens dial (Mara)
- **UI:** A modal showing a circular brass ring with eight etched panels (anchor, gull, star, bell, ship, fish, key, crown). A fixed pointer at the bottom is marked WELL. Arrow buttons and the A/D keys rotate the ring one panel per press with a soft clunk. A grey soot overlay covers the centre until the **Wipe with scarf** button is pressed. The wipe is a short drag animation, and the text reads: "The soot comes away in long streaks. Something scratched comes away with it. Gull grit, probably."
- **Solve:** Wiped, with the anchor under WELL. A chime plays and the modal closes with "The lens settles. Far below, something brightens."
- **Wrong state:** Nothing fails. If the lens hasn't been wiped, the plate stays a smear.

### Mini-puzzle B: Valve wheels (Tobin)
- **UI:** A modal with five iron wheels in a row, each with a coloured brass band. Clicking a wheel (or pressing keys 1–5) turns it a quarter turn and cycles its colour: red, blue, green, yellow, white. Below them is a large **Open sluice** lever.
- **Clue:** The plate shows WHITE, GREEN, RED, YELLOW, BLUE with a backwards "1" next to the right-hand dot. The logbook said "the well flips everything", so the true order from left to right is **BLUE, YELLOW, RED, GREEN, WHITE**.
- **Wrong:** The pipes bang, a hiss plays, the wheels keep their settings and the tide goes up by 1. Tobin: "Not that. The pipes are complaining."
- **Right:** The existing flood transition plays, followed by "Behind the wall, the tide wheel starts to turn."

### Mini-puzzle C: Morse shutter (Mara, climax)
- **UI:** A modal with a large shutter lever. A short click (under 250 ms) is a dot and a hold is a dash. There are also **·** and **—** buttons and keyboard input (`.` and `-`, or tap/hold Space). A reference card always shows the letters needed: C −·−·, R ·−·, A ·−, N −·, E ·, S ···, O −−−. The target word appears as letter boxes. Each box fills when its letter is entered correctly. A wrong symbol shakes and resets only the current letter. Each press flashes the lamp overlay in the window behind the modal.
- **Choice:** Before tapping, pick the message: **CRANE** (enabled only with the ledger in Mara's hands) or **SOS**.
- **Finish:** The cutter flashes back R (·−·) through the window, then the ending card plays.

### Tampering beats and payoffs
| Beat | Who | Why it looks innocent | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lens wipe | Mara (player clicks Wipe) | Needed so the projection is readable | The final initial: one dash survives and the second, if there was one, is gone |
| Diary gasket | Tobin (player uses the diary on the valves) | Needed to stop the leak; Elias taught it | The ledger's "T. — low nights" can never be checked against "E's orders" |
| Flood washes prints | Tobin (consequence of solving the valves) | The tide wheel needs it | Before the flood the stairs show "two sets of prints, one smaller, one set never comes back down". Both leads are slight. After the flood the prints are gone |
| Letter / envelope | Player choice | Self-protection | Changes T4, the ending lines and how T7 lands |

### Tide clock
- **State:** `tide` runs from 0 to 6, shown as a small brass gauge in the top bar.
- **Advances:** +1 for every 3 minutes of active play (paused during modals, talks and the intro) and +1 for each wrong valve attempt. It caps at 6 and the player can never lose.
- **What it changes:**
  - Window: at tide 0–1 the low-tide art shows the reef and the Marigold's broken hull ("Danny."). At 2–3 it shows surf. At 4 and above it shows `lamp_after` storm waves, and the line "The reef is gone under black water. That's when it kills."
  - Cellar: before the flood, a translucent water line overlay creeps up the floor at each level.
  - Talks: from tide 4, Tobin's lines gain an urgency variant ("It's over my boots, Mara.").
  - Ending: the tide band (section 3) is locked in when `lamp_full` is set.
- **Tuning target:** An average player reaches full power at tide 3–4. Tune on Oct 9.

### Scope risks and cut order
The main risks are the Morse modal (input timing feels bad on trackpads, so the buttons and keys exist as a fallback), dialogue volume (keep every talk to six lines or fewer) and overlay alignment on the painted rooms (use the existing layout tool, now on the L key).

## Implementation notes

Built as designed, with all 20 steps, the seven talks, all three mini-puzzles, the tide clock and the hint ladder. Nothing from the cut order was needed. Decisions taken: 1928 and "the Triangle"; the last line is spoken by whichever character the player is *not* controlling at the hatch; Tobin is Elias's apprentice; the killer stays ambiguous. Deviations:

- **Final scene.** The lens streak and the scarf lines are combined into one lamp click for Mara. There is no scarf item. That click is optional, and the hatch line needs only the plate.
- **Ending flow.** The ending card, the cutter's R and "But that was later." play inside the game scene, so the final scene can follow. The Ending scene shows only "The tide went out at six." and the title frame (`ending_open`).
- **Tide.** The tide freezes when `lamp_full` is set, so the band in the ending matches what the player saw. Before the flood, Tobin's wade depth follows the tide level. Talk urgency lines start at tide 4.
- **Triggers.** T1 needs both `read_logbook` and `knows_code`. Hidden letters and envelopes are removed from the inventory. Mara's letter uses the existing `page_a` art, and the envelope's "T." is drawn in code.
- **Cellar.** The door hotspot became **stairs**, which show the boot prints before the flood and nothing after it. The rheostat hotspot and prop appear only after `lamp_lit`. The glass plate and everything on it (smear, off-centre light, dots, mirrored soot writing) are drawn in Phaser rather than painted.
- **Compass.** There is no compass hotspot. It is mentioned in the empty-drawer text.
- **Art.** Six new images (`lamp_lowtide`, `envelope`, `diary`, `rheostat`, `lens_closeup`, `ending_open`) for 10 credits. `lamp_lowtide` needed one retry. The rheostat cutout was recut by hand.
- **Opening cutscene.** The intro text became a skippable cutscene of about 37 seconds (`CutsceneScene`, data in `src/data/cutscenes.js`). It has five painted shots with slow pans, letterbox bars, rain and typed captions, and the captions are the intro text above split into short lines. Four new stills are edits of `title` and `cellar_before` (5 credits, including one rejected retry), and the last shot pans down the title cutaway from the lamp room to the cellar. It plays from the title screen, so "Play again" shows it too. The tide clock and the play timer start only after it ends.
- **Pacing.** The tide step is still the proposed 3 minutes. It has not been tuned against real players yet.

### Engagement pass (Oct 6)

Added after the base game was complete. It needed no new art (0 credits).

- **Case board** (`src/data/board.js`, `UIScene.renderBoard`). The notebook is now the second tab of a "Case" modal (C opens the board, N opens the notes). Each clue is also a card with a title, a one-line text, and either an item icon or a drawn glyph. The four columns are Crane, Mara, Tobin and the Triangle. You can drag a card, or click it and then click a column (1-4 and 0 also work), and you can move it back to the tray. State keeps `board[id] = {column, fresh, order}`. A card arriving after the first swap shows the toast "Added to the case board" and a NEW tag that clears when the board closes. Pinning certain cards to certain columns gets one remark in the voice of the character you're playing (`REACTIONS`). Each remark plays once. They hint at motive or doubt and never confirm the killer. `boardVerdict` picks one epilogue line for the Ending scene, using these rules:
  - A column wins if it has at least two cards, at least half the pinned cards, and more than any other column.
  - If the Tobin or Mara column wins while the other lead's column is empty, the line becomes "You never doubted ...".
  - Otherwise, three or more columns in use gives "everyone", and anything else stays open.

  The final-plate step adds a toast reminding the player that the board is still open.
- **Tube choices** (`choice` entries in `TALKS`, picked with 1-3). There are four choices: Tobin's alibi and Mara's alibi in T1, the rheostat in T6 and the ledger in T7. The options are come clean (+1 trust), deflect (0) and lie (-1). Each concealment choice also moves trust (told +1, hidden -1). The canonical lines from the draft are now the lie options, so the story facts never change and no option blocks progress. What trust changes:
  - At warm trust (2 or more), Tobin volunteers the ledger "T." in T5.
  - Mara's T4 reply depends on her alibi choice.
  - The two closing lines of T7 are phrased from the trust level after the choice.
  - Some cards come from one choice only:
    - Tobin's "aunt" alibi comes only from the lie.
    - "Back at 11:40", "Thursday visit", "E's orders" and "Crane's offer" come only from coming clean.
    - Mara's "never" alibi comes only from the lie.
- **Final lines.** Each of the two final lines now has a warm and a cold version, chosen by trust (`finalLine`). Both versions keep the soot detail.
- **Two hands, moment A.** Bringing the lamp to full power is now a timed hold. Tobin's rheostat starts an 8-second hold (`state.holding`). A ring and an outline count down on the swap button, a top-right bar shows the time left, and the clock ticks every second. Mara's lamp click latches the lamp only while the hold is active. When time runs out, the rheostat slips back with a line for whoever is active, plus a queued remark for Tobin. You can retry at once. The first slip costs +1 tide. The hold clock pauses while a modal is open, and the holder's other clicks are refused.
- **Two hands, moment B.** Once the valves are set, lightning strikes every 20-30 seconds. It flashes, plays thunder, and shows "TWO BOATS / THURS" in the salt on Mara's window for 3 seconds. If Mara is in the lamp room, she sees it and gets the "Two boats" card. Strikes stop once the writing is seen, and seeing it is never required. The window hint also points at it.
- **Reactive world.**
  - `CROSS_ROOM` reactions are queued for the other character and spoken on the next swap. Some also play a muffled line through the tube straight away.
  - Used-up hotspots cycle through `BARKS` between repeats of their normal text.
  - After 25 seconds without input, the character says an idle mutter (`IDLE_MUTTERS`, in three tide bands). The same line never plays twice in a row, and mutters wait until no modal, talk or message is showing.
  - Lamp flicker, rain inside the window, storm volume (`Sfx.setStorm`) and camera sway all grow with the tide, and mute still applies.

### Movement, onboarding and new visuals (Oct 7)

- **Walking.** Each room has a `floor: { minX, maxX, y }` in `rooms.js`. Clicking empty space walks there; A/D or the arrows walk while held. Clicking a hotspot walks to its optional `stand` x (otherwise beside it, on the near side), turns to face it, then uses it with the item that was selected at click time. A new click retargets the walk, and world clicks are ignored while a modal is open. Speed is 280 px/s. The two side-on walk frames alternate at 5 fps with a small bob. The design said to use idle as the passing frame, but the idle art faces the viewer, so it only stands in when walk art is missing. Walk and talk frames are fitted to the idle pose's height, and a crouch to 68% of it. Hotspots whose centre is below y=430 are used crouching, the rest with the act pose, and tube talks use the talk pose facing the hatch. `CHARACTERS[who].faces` says which way each pose's art faces; idle is never flipped. Each character's x is kept across swaps. The rheostat holder can't walk. Walking is presentation only: handlers, state and the headless test never see it, and automation sets `window.__fastWalk` (on under WebDriver) to teleport. The layout tool moved from D to **L** and now draws and sets the floor line.
- **Making the start evident.** A "How to play" card with six illustrated rows follows the opening. It is remembered across replays (`seen.howto` survives `reset`), and ?, F1 or the ? button reopen it. Each hint step has an `objective` per character, shown as "Objective: ..." under the name in the top bar and pulsed when it changes. The test checks both characters have one at every step on four branches, and that it changes whenever the step does. Holding Space or Shift outlines and labels every hotspot; a top-bar tip advertises it for the first four minutes or until used. On the first visit to each room the hotspots shimmer in turn and an arrow bounces over the step's `target` hotspot until it is clicked or the objective moves on.
- **Close-ups.** The logbook, chalk, letter, ledger (at the plank) and boot prints (stairs, before the flood) open a painted close-up with the writing laid over it in Georgia italic (`CLOSEUPS` in `text.js`; the chalk anchor is drawn in code). Handlers call `api.closeup(id, then)`, which is a no-op in the test. Messages wait until the close-up is closed.
- **Beats.** `api.cutscene('lamplit')` plays when the lamp first reaches full power, and the truth ending plays `arrest` (via `ENDINGS.truth.beat`) after the cutter's reply and before the ending card. `CutsceneScene` in `beat` mode sleeps Game and UI and wakes them afterwards without touching state; the tide and hold clocks are paused while asleep. A beat whose still and fallback are both missing is skipped.
- **Portraits.** Painted portraits (`assets/portraits/`) replace the cropped sprite heads in the talk panel and appear beside the concealment choices. Worried variants, if present, are used at wary trust, from tide 4, on lines that open with "..." and for the concealment choices. Otherwise the code falls back to the normal portrait, then to the crop.
